ESP32-C3 vs ESP32-S3: which one should your next build use?

PUBLISHED JUN 28, 2026 READING TIME 5 MIN TAG GUIDE

Every week someone asks me which ESP32 to buy, and every week the honest answer is “it depends” — so here is the depends, written down. The classic WROOM-32 is still fine, but for new designs the real decision in 2026 is between the little C3 and the muscular S3. They are much less interchangeable than the naming suggests.

The short version

The C3 is a single RISC-V core at 160 MHz with about 400 KB of SRAM, Wi-Fi 4 and Bluetooth 5 LE, and — the killer feature — native USB serial/JTAG on the chip itself. It is tiny, it is cheap, and boards like the C3 SuperMini cost less than a decent coffee. The S3 is a dual-core Xtensa LX7 at 240 MHz with 512 KB of SRAM, optional PSRAM in the module, USB OTG, vector instructions for on-device AI, and enough GPIO to drive cameras and parallel LCDs.

Best forSensors, buttons, small nodesCameras, displays, audio, USB devices

When the C3 wins

Sensor nodes, smart buttons, relays, the little always-on things a smart home is actually made of. A C3 SuperMini soldered behind a light switch is doing exactly the same job an S3 would — for a third of the price and half the standby power. If your firmware is “read a thing, publish over MQTT, sleep”, buying an S3 is just donating money to a chip that will spend its life idle.

When the S3 wins

The moment pixels, sound or USB enter the project. Camera streaming, a touch display dashboard, a BadUSB-style HID gadget, TinyML keyword spotting — the S3 does all of these comfortably and the C3 does none of them well. The second core also matters more than benchmarks suggest: keeping Wi-Fi housekeeping on one core while your timing-critical code owns the other is what makes LED and audio projects stop glitching.

My rule

Default to the C3 and let the project argue you up to the S3 — never the other way around.

Nine of the last ten nodes I deployed at home are C3s. The tenth is an S3 driving a camera — because that one genuinely needed it.
